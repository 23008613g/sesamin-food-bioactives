# Dietary physiological context from the original rat study

Source: Ide T, Lim JS, Odbayar TO, Nakashima Y (2009), *J Nutr Sci Vitaminol* 55:31–43. DOI: [10.3177/jnsv.55.31](https://doi.org/10.3177/jnsv.55.31). Experiment 1 used male Sprague-Dawley rats fed a lignan-free diet or 0.2% sesamin for 15 days. These are original-study observations, not newly measured or recomputed phenotypes.

Values are mean ± SE, **7 animals per group**. The transcriptome comprises 5 selected animals per group, not all 7. No animal-level phenotype-to-array join is available in the accessed materials; correlation with VNN1 was not calculated.

Suggested bounded discussion sentence: “In the original dietary experiment, sesamin was associated with lower group-level hepatic triacylglycerol despite the positive VNN1 transcript difference observed in our reanalysis. Because phenotype records could not be linked to individual arrays, this juxtaposition provides physiological context rather than evidence that VNN1 mediates the lipid change.”

Interpretation boundary: this is dietary exposure in an experimental rat setting, not a demonstrated treatment of human fatty liver. The dietary percentage is not translated into a human sesame intake or an effective food dose. The comparison cannot isolate species, exposure route, disease status or assay platform as the cause of a cross-study difference.
